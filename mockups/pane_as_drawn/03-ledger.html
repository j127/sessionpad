The pane as a book of accounts. Every Text passes mono, rows are stripes in fixed-width columns, the note actions shrink to H, E and X pills explained by one key line, deleted notes wait on a shelf, and the headline sits in a tinted strip. Each piece is a Box, Text, Button or Input the mod can pass today.

accent theme key: Desktop frames follow the app's light or dark mode Terminal frames are always the dark terminal

Desktop app the Code tab's side pane, 400px wide

Desktop. Every Text passes mono; buttons and the field are drawn by the app, so their labels keep its own face. Columns line up only because each one is a Box of fixed width with flexShrink={0}; runs of spaces would collapse. Stripes are promptBorder, the edit block is a diffAddedDimmed rectangle and the shelf diffRemovedDimmed, the only shades that follow the app into dark mode. A row centres its cells, so a two-line value pulls its label to its middle.

Terminal the same tree in a dark terminal, 56 columns

Terminal. One font, one size, so mono, size and weight change nothing: the day rows, section heads and headline pass bold here instead. Stripes, the headline strip and the shelf all use subtle, since a terminal has no soft green or pink; the edit block becomes a round frame in the accent. Tabs carry their hotkey digit, and a dim rule of cells sits under them. Cells sit at the top of their row.

Band above the prompt one dim line, both surfaces

Band. <Text dimColor wrap="truncate-end">pad: {headline}</Text>, unchanged from the pane the mod draws now. It stays in the app's own face: the ledger's monospace belongs to the pane.

Design notes — 03 · Ledger

Direction

Ledger is the dense, aligned option and leans terminal. It reads like a book of accounts: monospace throughout, every list a set of striped rows in fixed columns, and the three note actions cut to H, E and X pills with one key line above the list to say what they do, since hints on a Button's hover don't show. Deleted notes go to a Recently deleted shelf with a Restore button instead of vanishing, and the headline is a tinted strip with an accent pad label at the top of Notes.

Typography

Inside the frames the only type choices are the mod's own props: mono on every Text, weight="semibold" for the headline, day rows and section heads, size="footnote" for meta lines, key lines and marks, and italic for the aside. Buttons and the field are drawn by the app in its own face, so "Export" or "save" never turn monospace. The panel's font (System or Serif), radius and density change only this page's chrome: the header, the captions and these notes.

Palette

A palette is the accent theme key the pane passes as color and borderColor: claude orange, merged purple or success green. It colors the pad label, the ›, code spans in notes, the branch mark, the open terminal tab and the terminal's edit frame. Primary buttons stay black or white whatever the accent, because the app draws them. The only fills are promptBorder, diffAddedDimmed and diffRemovedDimmed on Desktop, which follow the mode, and subtle in a terminal.

What differs between the surfaces

  • Tabs: a black or white pill for the open tab on Desktop; in a terminal, hotkey digits, the open tab bold, underlined and in the accent, the rest dim, and a dim rule of cells under them.
  • Fills: Desktop's stripes, headline strip and day rows are promptBorder; the terminal's are subtle. The edit block is a soft green rectangle on Desktop and a round accent frame in a terminal; the shelf is soft pink on Desktop and subtle in a terminal.
  • Type: sizes, weights and mono only draw on Desktop. Where Desktop uses semibold, the terminal gets bold. The aside's italic draws only on Desktop.
  • Alignment: a Desktop row centres its cells, so a label sits by the middle of a two-line value; terminal cells sit at the top.
  • Controls: pills and a bordered field on Desktop; [ label ] and › Add a note… on one line in a terminal, where the rows={3} field is three lines with an inverse cursor.
  • Width: at 400px the Desktop summary column is about 14 cells, so long summaries wrap to four or five lines; the 56-column terminal pane gives it 27.

Left out because the pane can't draw it

  • Code chips inside notes: Markdown has no mono, so a note is split into mono Text runs and code becomes accent text.
  • Hover hints on H, E and X: the key line does their job.
  • Rails, frames and table rules made of line characters on Desktop: tables are Boxes, and the only rule is the terminal's.
  • Top-aligned cells and vertical padding inside a stripe on Desktop: only line height gives a stripe room.
  • Italic in a terminal.
